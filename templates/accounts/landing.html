Photo meal logging

Snap a photo, get calories and macros back instantly.

No manual entry. Take a picture of your plate and the system identifies the food and estimates nutrition automatically.

How this works
clip-vit-base-patch32 linear probing

We use OpenAI's CLIP Vision Transformer as a frozen feature extractor, and fine-tuned a linear classification head on top of it using supervised learning on a 10-class food dataset (500 images, 80/20 train-val split). Training the full ViT was not feasible on limited hardware, so only the small linear head was trained via backpropagation — a standard transfer-learning technique. Validation accuracy reached ~90%. For foods outside these 10 trained classes, we fall back to CLIP's zero-shot classification against a wider food list.

Text meal logging

Describe your meal in plain language.

Type something like "2 rotis and dal, felt tired" and the system extracts individual food items and quantities.

How this works
all-MiniLM-L6-v2 BERT-family sentence embeddings

We use a pretrained BERT-family sentence-transformer to embed your text and compare it semantically against known food names, rather than exact keyword matching. This is zero-shot — no fine-tuning was applied to this component, which we note transparently since it differs from the vision model above.

Conversational coach

Ask questions, grounded in real facts.

Chat with a coach that answers from a curated knowledge base rather than making things up.

How this works
gemini-3.5-flash RAG

Every question is matched against a small curated set of nutrition/fitness facts using semantic search, and only the most relevant facts are passed to Gemini as grounding context — this is Retrieval-Augmented Generation. Common factual questions are answered instantly from a hardcoded lookup with no API call at all, and repeated questions are served from a cache, to reduce cost and avoid unnecessary generation.

Adaptive planning agent

Your plan updates itself, and explains why.

Every week, an agent reviews your weight trend and adherence, decides whether to adjust your calorie target, and explains the decision in plain language.

How this works
rule-based decision logic gemini-3.5-flash for explanation only

The decision itself (increase, decrease, maintain, or flag for human review) is deterministic rule-based logic over your weight trend and adherence — this keeps the core behavior debuggable and predictable. An LLM is used only to translate that decision into a warm, plain-language explanation, and only when the decision is non-trivial; simple "maintain" outcomes use a fixed template instead, avoiding an unnecessary API call.
